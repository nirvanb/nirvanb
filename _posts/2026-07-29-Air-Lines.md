In the past few weeks, wildfires have raged across the globe. In North America, Canada burns from coast to coast, while active fires plague every state east of the Dakotas. Even in Europe, [nearly 300,000 people](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/29/g-s1-136057/france-wildfires) in France, Spain, and Portugal evacuated as a result of fires. Such fires have become commonplace in our warming world, fueled by droughts and heat waves. They also make me shockingly popular for a few days each year, as smoke descends on Michigan and my phone is flooded with photos of a hazy orange sun and skylines lost to soupy atmosphere.


![Sky in Saline, MI. PC Dad]({{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/images/post_img/SalineSmokeSun.jpg){:style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto"; width="600" height="400"}

Wildfire smoke is a concern in itself, leading to increases in ER visits and mortality.[^1][^2] Harm reduction can include masking outdoors, air purifiers ([including effective home-made options](https://www.npr.org/2025/06/08/nx-s1-5257523-e1/making-diy-air-filters-wildfire-smoke)), and improving filtration in HVAC systems, a list I dutifully regurgitate to everyone who texts me. These now-annual intrusions of smoke have brought the reality of wildfire closer, reinforcing how pollution anywhere quickly becomes pollution everywhere. Even halfway around the globe, we regularly see North American smoke and Saharan dust at research stations.

![Detroit Skyline in Smoke. PC C. Hibbard]({{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/images/post_img/DetroitSmokeSkyline.jpg){:style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto"; width="600" height="400"}

Except, the idea that ["air pollution knows no borders"](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/video/air-pollution-knows-no-borders-so-nations-must-work-together-un-secretary) seems not to have penetrated Capitol Hill. Michigan Rep John James responded to the fires looking to [impose sanctions](https://eu.detroitnews.com/story/news/local/michigan/2026/07/17/canada-wildfire-smoke-michigan-john-james-matt-hall-sanctions-hearings/90953021007/) on Canada. He was joined by the president, who called for [additional tariffs](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cwyq93j34lgo), his foreign policy hammer that makes everything look like a nail. 

The response of the Canadian government was, as you might expect, anger and bafflement. The US has moved further and further away from climate commitments, destroyed scientific expertise, and taken a hostile stance toward international cooperation at every turn. Moving forward, we need to look back toward the [rich history of international agreements](https://openaq.medium.com/air-knows-no-borders-the-state-of-transnational-air-quality-standards-b0ba7f0d8665) on the air and how they have improved our atmosphere. Agreements like the [1979 Convention on Long-Range Transboundary Air Pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_Long-Range_Transboundary_Air_Pollution) (CLRTAP) established protocols to reduce emissions and concentrations of sulfur, heavy metals, ammonia, amd more. Furthermore, this agreement standardized data collection, sharing, and coordinated monitoring. The [European Monitoring and Evaluation Programme](https://www.emep.int/) (EMEP) continues to operate today, and their shared EBAS databases and protocols support other monitoring networks, including the ACTRIS network I am involved in.[^3] Famously, the [Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer](https://www.unep.org/ozonaction/who-we-are/about-montreal-protocol)[^4] helped us reduce emissions of CFCs and [close a hole in the ozone layer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-48625-z) far above us. If we can do this, we can work together to breathe a little less smoke and lose fewer forests and homes to flames.

There are certainly mechanisms for it! The [Canada-US Air Quality Agreement](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/air-pollution/issues/transboundary/canada-united-states-air-quality-agreement-overview.html) comes to mind. Since 1991, it has successfully reduced emissions and formation of SO<sub>2</sub>, NO<sub>X</sub>, and ozone. Washington should consider expanding these committments to include cooperation on the changing climate, forest management, and new building codes to reduce wildfire damage and smoke harm. But I think we'll all be left holding our breath.
