Provence takes up a lot of space in the culinary psyche, a blend of simple cookery with liberal use of tomatoes, garlic, olive oil, and herbs.<!--more--> Luminary French chefs like Alain Ducasse claim the region and present their take on the cuisine, as do various international chefs like Thomas Keller and [Heston Blumenthal](https://guide.michelin.com/en/article/travel/heston-blumenthal-provence-restaurants). Heeding the grumbling siren song of my stomach. I decided to take a short trip to the region, a 2.5 day detour on my way to Geneva for the CLOUD campaign.

<img src="https://nirvanb.github.io/nirvanb/images/post_img/Avignon.jpeg" alt="Avignon, photo Nirvan Bhattacharyya" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto" width="300">

I made my way to Avignon, the small walled city known as the City of Popes, nestled in the curving meanders of the Rhône before it joins with the Durance. Here, I toured the *Palais des Papes*, the home of the Avignon Papacy in the 14th century. The Gothic edifice rises from cobbled plaza among the rolling hills of Provence. Walking these halls is strange, a reminder of a time when the force of the Church was both stronger and weaker, and the ambitions of theological supremacy more to the fore. Taken by the Napoleonic State as a barracks and prison, most statues of saints and other religious icons are beheaded, though beautiful frescos endure in the Saint-Martial and Saint-Jean chapels.

<img src="https://nirvanb.github.io/nirvanb/images/post_img/PalacePopes.jpeg" alt="Palace of the Popes, photo Nirvan Bhattacharyya" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto" width="300">

To treat myself to Provence culinary delights in style, I went to [*Le Goût du Jour*](https://legoutdujour84.com/)[^0], a modern, yet cozy spot run by Avignon chef Julian Chazal. In a failed attempt at moderation, I opted for the 4 course menu with wine pairings[^1]. The meal opened with a quartet of *amuse-bouche* all playing with herbal, grassy flavors, starting with a spoon of ice flavored with fire-y herbs, then onto a grassy tempura-fried shiso leaf dusted in wasabi powder. The long almond biscuit was covered in *pistou*, Provence's answer to pesto. Without the pine nuts or cheese, the bitter vegetal notes of basil come more to the fore, as does the bite of freshly crushed garlic. Finally, a small round of homemade, nutty bread served with sage butter closed this course. (An additional boule of crusty, part-wheat bread was provided for finishing up the butter and mopping up sauces throughout the meal.)

<img src="https://nirvanb.github.io/nirvanb/images/post_img/GoutAmuse.jpeg" alt="Amuse Bouche, photo Nirvan Bhattacharyya" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto" width="300">

The starter looks at first glance like dessert, consisting of terrine of roasted and compressed beetroot, perched atop a savory shortbread biscuit. A smooth gelée of beetroot and blackberry crowned this, adorned with picked blackberries, dollops of creme fraiche and thin wedges of watermelon radish. Served with a blackberry-beetroot puree and a sorbet of the same, this course was earthy, bracing, and delightful.

<img src="https://nirvanb.github.io/nirvanb/images/post_img/GoutStarter.jpeg" alt="Starter, photo Nirvan Bhattacharyya" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto" width="300">

The fish and meat courses both paired rich meats and fruits. The first was seared tuna served with figs, both roasted and in a vinaigrette, alongside long rectangular potato gnocchi and a small mount of intensely acidic pickled roots. 

<img src="https://nirvanb.github.io/nirvanb/images/post_img/GoutTuna.jpeg" alt="Tuna, photo Nirvan Bhattacharyya" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto" width="300">

Next, duck was paired with quince and coffee, presented two ways. First, a blushing pink slice of duck breast, served with roasted and pickled quince, a coffee *sabayon*, and a rich jus. Next, perched atop a small tabletop barbecue of some sort, confit duck was shredded and fried in a tempura batter, while below a small wooden ramekin of coffee and quince barbecue sauce was ready for dipping. All three plates felt balanced and indulgent. Even one of them would have had me talking for ages about them, though I think the tuna and figs is the one I am most likely to recreate myself.

<img src="https://nirvanb.github.io/nirvanb/images/post_img/GoutDuck.jpeg" alt="Tuna, photo Nirvan Bhattacharyya" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto" width="300">

Dessert was as visually striking as the rest, with a bright red raspberry shell encasing a decadent chocolate filling, paired with a smooth raspberry sorbet and a bitter beer jelly. The sorbet and jelly eaten together reminded me of the excellent Nitro Rubaeus beer produced by Founders Brewing, which I drank regularly most summers in college. While not my favorite dessert, it was still a lovely and striking capstone to an excellent meal.

<img src="https://nirvanb.github.io/nirvanb/images/post_img/GoutDessert.jpeg" alt="Dessert, photo Nirvan Bhattacharyya" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto" width="300">

The next morning, I took a short train to Arles, another walled city in the region, famous for inspiring many of Van Gogh's great works. Here, I walked through the Coliseum, smaller than the one in Rome, but no less grand to my eyes. It is even still in use for concerts and events! I sat in its shadow to enjoy a lunch of razor clams, shrimp with a charred leek risotto, and a pistachio lava cake. So encumbered, I took a long walk around some local parks and along the Rhône, to that bend in the river said to inspire Van Gogh's Starry Night.

<img src="https://nirvanb.github.io/nirvanb/images/post_img/Arles.png" alt="Arles, photo Nirvan Bhattacharyya" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto" width="300">

My first morning in Provence, I ran into a friendly French woman in her 50s named Asia. Upon learning I was a visitor, she insisted on powerwalking me through town to the best breakfast place she knew. During our brief meeting, she asked me only one question I could not answer, "Where is your heart?" For her, she said, "My heart is here! In France! In Provence!" I do not know where my heart is, but Provence would not be a bad place to leave it.
